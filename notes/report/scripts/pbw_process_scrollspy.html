Step 1 — Substrate preparation

Metal seed layer and Si wafer

The process begins with a polished silicon wafer onto which a thin metal seed layer is deposited. The seed layer — typically Cr, Ti, Pd, or Au — serves as the conductive base required for subsequent electroplating.

Selection of the seed layer material affects adhesion to the substrate and the quality of the electrodeposited metal. The wafer is cleaned prior to deposition to minimise surface contamination.

Step 2 — Resist spin coating

PMMA resist deposition

A resist layer is applied to the seed layer surface by spin coating. The thickness of the resist determines the height of the final metal features and must be carefully controlled.

PMMA is the typical resist used in PBW processes due to its sensitivity to MeV protons and its ability to produce high-aspect-ratio structures with smooth sidewalls after development.

Step 3a — PBW exposure

Proton beam writing

A focused MeV-energy proton beam is scanned in a predetermined grid pattern over the resist surface. Due to the greater mass of protons relative to electrons, the beam travels in a near-linear trajectory through the resist with minimal lateral scattering.

Secondary electrons generated by the proton-resist interaction have energies typically below 100 eV, limiting their range to within a few nanometres of the proton track — resulting in minimal proximity effects and near-vertical sidewalls.
Step 3b — Resist development

Chemical development

Following exposure, the resist is chemically developed. In the exposed regions, the proton beam has modified the polymer chain structure, making those regions preferentially soluble in the developer solution.

The result is a patterned resist mould with the grid geometry defined by the PBW scan pattern. Sidewall quality at this stage directly determines the fidelity of the final metal grid.

Step 4 — Metal deposition and liftoff

Ni electroplating and PMMA removal

Ni sulfamate electroplating is performed to fill the developed resist mould, producing a nickel grid from the patterned template. The seed layer acts as the cathode during electrodeposition.

Following plating, the remaining PMMA resist is removed by liftoff, leaving the freestanding nickel grid features on the seed layer surface. The seed layer beneath non-feature regions may then be etched away to produce the final freestanding grid.

Analysis — Characterisation

AFM and SEM metrology

The fabricated grid is characterised using AFM tapping mode and scanning electron microscopy. Sidewall angle is extracted from cross-sectional AFM profiles and compared against the ≥89.4° benchmark.

Surface roughness (Rq) is measured across multiple regions to confirm uniformity. Results are validated against SRIM Monte Carlo simulations of the expected proton beam trajectory and energy deposition profile through the resist.
